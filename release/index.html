Insulation and air sealing: write two clear scopes

A room can feel uncomfortable for more than one reason. Ask the assessment to distinguish heat flow through the assembly from unwanted air movement, then connect the proposed work to the observed problem.

Read article →Field notes · 02

What to include in an attic insulation brief

An attic enquiry should describe access, existing conditions and the purpose of the work. Start with observations from a safe accessible position and leave investigation of concealed or hazardous conditions to the appropriate professional.

Read article →Field notes · 03

Crawl-space moisture before insulation

An insulation quote is not a diagnosis of a wet crawl space. Record the water history and ask for the cause to be assessed before deciding where insulation belongs or what material should be used.

Read article →Field notes · 04

Preparing for a rim-joist insulation assessment

The perimeter where a floor meets an exterior wall is often congested with services and framing. Give the assessor a clear picture of those junctions before asking for a material or thickness.

Read article →Field notes · 05

Planning insulation below a room over a garage

A cold room above a garage needs an assessment of the whole floor assembly. The project brief should identify comfort symptoms, services, existing finishes and any separation requirements that the work could disturb.

Read article →Field notes · 06

Comparing insulation materials for the actual assembly

A material comparison is useful only when each option is being considered for the same job. Define the assembly and project constraints before comparing foam, batts, blown materials or spray-applied fibre.

Read article →Field notes · 07

Site access questions before insulation work

Good access information helps a contractor price the work accurately and plan disruption. Describe the route into the building and the conditions at the work area, including obstacles that do not appear on a floor plan.

Read article →Field notes · 08

Planning an insulation project in an occupied home

Work in an occupied home needs a clear schedule for access, separation and return to use. Ask the contractor to explain the arrangements for the selected product and actual installation conditions.

Read article →Field notes · 09

Coordinate insulation before renovation finishes

Renovations offer access to parts of a building that may soon be concealed. Use that window to coordinate insulation, services and inspections so the finished surfaces do not need to be reopened unnecessarily.

Read article →Field notes · 10

What to record when condensation is the concern

Condensation is a symptom to investigate, not a product specification. A useful enquiry describes when and where moisture appears and supplies the building history needed for an assessment.

Read article →Field notes · 11

Insulation quote exclusions worth resolving

Two insulation prices can cover different amounts of work even when the headline area is identical. Review the exclusions while changes can still be priced and scheduled clearly.

Read article →Field notes · 12

Build an insulation project document folder

A simple project folder makes it easier to compare proposals and maintain the building later. Organise records by purpose so the final installation can be distinguished from early options.

Read article →Field notes · 13

Photographs that improve an insulation estimate

Photos can make an initial discussion more useful when they show context as well as detail. They support early planning but do not replace an assessment of hidden conditions or a measured final scope.

Read article →Field notes · 14

Review insulation work before it is concealed

A review before finishes are installed gives the project team an opportunity to resolve questions while the work is accessible. Arrange that review in the programme rather than treating it as an optional final photograph.
