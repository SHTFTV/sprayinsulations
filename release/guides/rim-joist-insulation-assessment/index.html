Preparing for a rim-joist insulation assessment

By Industry Army Marketing (IAM) · September 28, 2026

The perimeter where a floor meets an exterior wall is often congested with services and framing. Give the assessor a clear picture of those junctions before asking for a material or thickness.

Map the accessible perimeter

Identify finished and unfinished sections, plumbing and electrical penetrations, and areas hidden by ceilings or storage. Photographs should include surrounding framing and wall connections. Mark anything that cannot be inspected without opening finishes.

Review the complete junction

Ask how the proposed work connects with the wall and floor insulation and air-control layers. Include evidence of moisture or previous repairs. The correct detail depends on the building assembly, so a photograph from another house should be treated as a discussion aid rather than a construction instruction.

Clarify reinstatement and records

Agree who removes and reinstates finishes, what clearances remain around services and which inspections are required before concealment. Request labelled completion photographs of otherwise hidden sections. Keep product identification and the accepted scope with the building records.

Keep with your project brief

  • Accessible perimeter map
  • Junction and moisture assessment
  • Concealment and reinstatement plan
