Materials & legacy / HiBar fireproofing: images and project context

HiBar is a separate material topic

SOPREMA describes HiBAR as a spray-applied blend of mineral fibres and binders. Its manufacturer page supplies the current product and technical documents. Foam photographs do not illustrate this material and are not used as substitutes here.

Start with the assembly records

For an existing building, collect drawings, the named product, any assembly reference and previous inspection or repair records. Show the structural members, decking, services and access constraints in your own photographs.

Define the assessment and handover

Ask who will confirm the applicable system, preparation and inspection requirements. A written scope should identify the work areas, access arrangements, protection of surrounding surfaces and the records to be supplied at handover. A photo cannot establish a fire-resistance rating.

Questions before an assessment

Is this HiBar image from a Spray Insulations job?

No. It is credited manufacturer imagery from SOPREMA’s HiBAR product gallery.

Why is HiBar separate from spray foam?

The material and assembly must be identified separately; a foam project photograph is not a HiBar reference.

Where are the current product documents?

Use the linked SOPREMA HiBAR page for its technical and safety documents.

Can I determine the required thickness from this photo?

No. Ask the responsible professional to identify the actual assembly and applicable requirements.

What should a HiBar enquiry include?

Include the building use, work area, drawings or specification, access constraints and photographs of the actual condition.

Plan the work on your property

Send your project details and existing records to start an enquiry. Availability and the final scope must be confirmed with the responsible contractor.
