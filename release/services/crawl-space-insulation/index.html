INSULATION PLANNING

Crawl-space insulation: moisture and access first

Cold floors are a reason to investigate. The right scope starts with the crawl space beneath them, including water history, access and the location of services.

Prepare a useful description

You do not need to enter a difficult crawl space to make an enquiry. Describe the access hatch from a normal accessible position, approximate property age and the rooms above it. Mention standing water, seasonal dampness, plumbing leaks, odours and any previous work. Existing inspection photographs are more useful than a guess about the material you need.

Identify the proposed boundary

Ask the assessor to explain whether the project concerns the floor above, the foundation perimeter or another defined assembly. NRCan describes more than one approach to crawl-space insulation. The choice should be explained for your building, including its moisture conditions and services. Avoid buying a package based only on a product name.

Separate drainage from insulation

A proposal should say what water or moisture investigation is included and what needs separate attention. Do not treat insulation as proof that a drainage issue has been resolved. Ask how responsibility is divided if another contractor is involved, and whether any prerequisite work must be completed before installation can be scheduled.

Set out the handover

Request a marked scope, preparation and removal allowances, product identification, access requirements and photographs of completed accessible areas. Ask the contractor to explain any required protective covering for the proposed foam system. Record exclusions around mechanical equipment and future maintenance access. These details make competing proposals much easier to compare.

Further reading

Natural Resources Canada: Keeping the Heat In. Use the official guidance alongside project-specific advice, product instructions and local requirements.
