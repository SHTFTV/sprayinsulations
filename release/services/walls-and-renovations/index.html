INSULATION PLANNING

Wall insulation during renovations

Use the opportunity created by open finishes to plan continuity, coordination and documentation—not just cavity fill.

Define what is being opened

Tell the assessor whether the project is an addition, an interior renovation or exterior work. Provide drawings where available and identify occupied areas that need to stay usable. Distinguish walls that will be fully exposed from those that will remain finished. Access affects both the proposed method and the confidence of a preliminary estimate.

Agree the complete wall approach

NRCan discusses insulation in the context of wall renovations and additions. Ask your project team to describe the assembly and its connection to nearby roof, floor and foundation areas. Clarify which materials remain, which are replaced and who approves changes if the existing construction differs from the drawings.

Coordinate the trade sequence

An insulation scope should fit the timing of electrical, plumbing, window and finishing work. Establish who confirms readiness and how inaccessible or incomplete areas are handled. Ask whether preparation, protective finishes and reinstatement are included. These responsibilities are often more important to the final budget than a small difference in quoted material cost.

Leave a useful record

Request photographs and product information for work that will be concealed. Keep written approvals for substitutions and changes with the renovation documents. If the project includes a room above a garage or another unusual boundary, ask for that assembly to be named separately in the scope so it receives specific consideration rather than a generic wall allowance.

Further reading

Natural Resources Canada: Keeping the Heat In. Use the official guidance alongside project-specific advice, product instructions and local requirements.
